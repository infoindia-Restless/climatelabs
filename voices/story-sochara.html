Voices / Change stories / SOCHARA

From helplessness to action

Helping a south Bengaluru community see the links between water, health and climate change, and act on them.

PartnerSociety for Community Health Awareness, Research and Action (SOCHARA)
WhereAvalahalli and Anjanapura, south Bengaluru
FocusWater, health and climate
Youth FundPhase 1
The challenge

What young people were up against

In Anjanapura, 63% of households face a water crisis several times a month. Life revolves around when water might come. Residents felt helpless, and few saw the connections between their water, their health and a changing climate.

What changed

What the grant made possible

Over six months, research, focus groups and co-design produced booklets and short videos made with the community, in its own languages. Young mobilisers from the neighbourhood carried them door to door, and residents began to name their problems and raise them with the ward.

63%of households face water crises several times a month
95%of respondents said the tools helped them understand water, health and climate

Working through the local ecosystem

SOCHARA worked with Social Innovation Studio, Makkala Jagriti and Vismaya Kalike to understand how people in Anjanapura use and think about water. Early on, some residents were wary of outsiders who had made promises before. Repeated meetings, and a focus on what people actually needed, water and livelihoods, built trust.

Young mobilisers as messengers

Young people from the community mapped local problems and then went door to door with the materials, starting conversations. Sessions at the Yuva Cafe covered microplastics, a plogging drive at Avalahalli Lake and a photography competition on waste. Videos shared on WhatsApp travelled further than the planned sessions, and households that had been missed came looking for the materials.

What residents took away

One resident said her street had long lived with rashes, throat irritation and other illness without knowing water could be the cause, and that she would now tell everyone to call the ward number. Others began linking erratic weather to coughs, colds and fevers in their families.

“Community mobilisers truly are the new superheroes, ready to bring effective, lasting change.”Ankita Mirani, founder, Social Innovation Studio
