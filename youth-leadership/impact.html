Youth Leadership / Impact and learning

Impact and learning

What changed for young leaders and their communities, what we learned, and what comes next.

Programme impact

Youth-led action met real community needs, and built trust between young people, residents and civic bodies.

Youth-led action

Grassroots interventions designed and led by young people addressed real community needs.

Better segregation

Households segregated waste at source and open dumping fell in residential lanes, as sanitation workers confirmed.

Safer water

Cleaner storage, a protected tank and better hygiene practices for families and schools.

Community ownership

Residents took on follow-ups and upkeep, and partnerships with local institutions grew.

Beyond the numbers

Leadership, inclusion, lasting change

The Lab's results show up in people as much as in places.

Young leaders

Confidence and skills
Youth Climate Leaders grew in communication, facilitation, negotiation, documentation and problem-solving, and in confidence speaking with elders and institutions.

Gender and inclusion

Women as decision-makers
Women and adolescent girls led water literacy spaces, recognising their outsized role in household water. Teams worked across caste, class, religion and age with care and respect.

Sustainability

Habits that last
Segregation continued with follow-ups and visibly cleaner streets. Women's leadership made upkeep of shared water more likely, and young leaders left ready to keep acting.
Learning

What we learned

  • Behaviour change takes time. One-off awareness was not enough; repeated household follow-ups made the difference.
  • Pictures beat instructions. Wall paintings, posters and visual tools worked better than talk alone.
  • Women sustain water work. Women-only water literacy spaces were strengthened after seeing their central role.
  • Learning by seeing. Exposure visits, like the rainwater harvesting school, made ideas concrete.
  • Systems move slowly. Delays from civic bodies taught young leaders how ward-level governance really works.
Way forward

Where the Lab goes next

  • Extend the YCAL approach to more wards, adapted to each context
  • Link youth groups with ward-level governance platforms
  • Run longer programme cycles for deeper behaviour change
  • Formalise women's collectives in water and sanitation management
  • Build exposure visits and experiential learning into all youth climate programmes
