Youth Leadership / Our journey

Our journey

Three phases of the Youth Climate Action Lab, from building relationships to youth-led research to action on the ground.

Since November 2021

From a new city and a small group of young champions to a youth-led climate movement in Bengaluru.

The Youth Climate Action Lab began in November 2021 as Restless Development's part of the Fair Urban Transitions programme supported by Porticus. Bengaluru was a new city for us. Over three phases the Lab moved from building relationships, to youth-led research, to young people leading action on the ground.

Along the way it connected with other organisations in the city, including Mahila Housing Trust and Purpose, which later came together in the Fair Futures Forum.

Phase 1Nov 2021 to Oct 2022Building the Lab

Laying the foundations in a new city

After a four-month delay caused by Covid-19, the Lab recruited its first Youth Climate Champions and Youth Researchers. Champions spread across urban Bengaluru to map communities and the issues that climate change was shaping there, building trust before any data was collected. Youth Researchers focused on climate change and the livelihoods of daily wage workers.

With Sama Foundation, a Youth Resource Centre opened in DJ Halli, one of the city's largest notified slums with around 35,000 residents: a safe space for training, events and community gatherings, supported by two Youth Facilitators.

Youth Climate Champion

Devika Chhabra, DJ Halli

Devika chose water as her focus, seeing it as a strong signal of climate change. Interviews with community members showed that water often arrived mixed with sewage. Women who juggle housework and informal jobs became her strongest allies, so she shaped a women-led approach and planned to test water samples and take findings to the city water board.

“My interactions with the community have made me realise how misinterpreted the idea and the urgency of climate change is. I would like to change that through my work.”
Phase 2Nov 2022 to May 2023Youth-led research and a global voice

Gathering evidence, and taking it to the world stage

Champions formed teams of changemakers in their communities and trained them in climate change, advocacy, using data and campaigning. Together they ran the youth-led research across five communities on infrastructure and resilience, solid waste, water, and youth and livelihoods.

The Youth Resource Centre in DJ Halli reached around 500 young people, and the team developed a Youth Engagement Module to help young people understand climate change at ground level.

1,500+people reached through community sessions
800+community members engaged in the research
55young people delivering the programme
20organisations engaged for the youth coalition
  • COP27: Devika Chhabra represented the Lab at COP27 in Egypt in November 2022, speaking on transforming education for sustainable development through youth climate action, including with UNESCO's Assistant Director-General for Education.
  • Working with partners: joined Purpose's community walk with Alli Serona on road access and transport, and Ellara Bengaluru's Community Resource Forum on ward-level action plans.
  • Adapting: since young people were juggling study and work, the Lab shifted to working within existing communities and partner areas so they could stay engaged longer.
Youth Climate Champion

Mayur, Adigarahalli

As a teenager, Mayur joined a protest that saved 100 trees from a road widening. Now a biology student, he focused on solid waste, surveying his community on the health hazards of poor waste disposal and leading workshops on waste, livelihoods and deforestation. Making the workshops welcoming to women was the key to getting the community involved.

Phase 3Dec 2024 to Nov 2025Youth-led action

From evidence to action in Shanthinagar

With Grama Vikas, eight Youth Climate Leaders moved from research to action. After training, three months in the community and a youth hackathon, four teams tackled waste and water in Shanthinagar's settlements, working with BBMP, BWSSB, schools, women's collectives and faith institutions.
